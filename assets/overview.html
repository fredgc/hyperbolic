Hyperbolic Overview

The Hyperbolic Drawing Tool is an interactive canvas for constructing and exploring hyperbolic geometry within the PoincaréDisk model (𝔻={z∈ℂ:|z|<1}\mathbb{D} = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : |z| < 1\}).

In the Poincarédisk, all of neutral geometry (Euclid’s postulates without the parallel postulate) holds, while the parallel postulate fails: given a line LL and an external point P∉LP \notin L, there are infinitely many distinct lines through PP that never meet LL.

1. The Poincaré Disk Model

  • Points: All points in hyperbolic space correspond to points inside the open unit disk |z|<1|z| < 1. Points on the unit boundary circle |z|=1|z| = 1 are points at infinity (ideal boundary points ∂𝔻\partial \mathbb{D}).

  • Lines (Geodesics): Hyperbolic straight lines are represented by Euclidean circular arcs that intersect the unit circle at right angles (90∘90^\circ), as well as Euclidean diameters passing through the origin.

  • Conformal Angles: The Poincarédisk is conformal: the angle between two hyperbolic lines equals the Euclidean angle between their tangent vectors at the intersection point.

2. Hyperbolic Distance & Metric

As points approach the boundary of the unit disk (|z|→1|z| \to 1), hyperbolic distances grow exponentially toward infinity.

For any two points u,v∈𝔻u, v \in \mathbb{D} (treated as complex numbers x+iyx + iy), the hyperbolic distance d(u,v)d(u, v) is:

d(u,v)=arcosh(1+2|u−v|2(1−|u|2)(1−|v|2))=2artanh|u−v1−u‾v|d(u, v) = \operatorname{arcosh}\left(1 + \frac{2|u - v|^2}{(1 - |u|^2)(1 - |v|^2)}\right) = 2 \operatorname{artanh}\left|\frac{u - v}{1 - \bar{u}v}\right|

It is often convenient to work with the pseudo-hyperbolic distance t=tanh(d(u,v)/2)∈[0,1)t = \tanh(d(u, v) / 2) \in [0, 1), which has the simple algebraic form:

t=|u−v1−u‾v|t = \left|\frac{u - v}{1 - \bar{u}v}\right|

For a point at Euclidean radius r=|z|r = |z| from the origin:

ρ=ln(1+r1−r)=2artanh(r)⇔r=tanh(ρ/2)\rho = \ln\left(\frac{1+r}{1-r}\right) = 2\operatorname{artanh}(r) \iff r = \tanh(\rho / 2)

Because distance expands near the boundary circle, the edge of the disk is infinitely far away.

3. Hyperbolic Circles

A hyperbolic circle (the locus of all points Q∈𝔻Q \in \mathbb{D} at hyperbolic distance ρ\rho from a hyperbolic center P∈𝔻P \in \mathbb{D}) is an ordinary Euclidean circle contained strictly inside the unit disk!

However, unless PP is at the origin, the Euclidean center CEC_E of that circle is shifted toward the origin relative to the true hyperbolic center PP because of the metric gradient across the disk.

Specifically, given hyperbolic center PP with p=|P|p = |P| and hyperbolic radius ρ\rho with t=tanh(ρ/2)t = \tanh(\rho / 2):

  • Euclidean center:

    CE=1−t21−p2t2PC_E = \frac{1 - t^2}{1 - p^2 t^2} P

  • Euclidean radius:

    RE=(1−p2)t1−p2t2R_E = \frac{(1 - p^2) t}{1 - p^2 t^2}

4. Straightedge and Compass Constructions

Classical straightedge-and-compass constructions adapt naturally to hyperbolic geometry:

Hyperbolic Compass

  • Center + Point on Circle (Collapsible Compass): Given hyperbolic center PP and passing point QQ, compute t=|P−Q1−P‾Q|t = \left|\frac{P - Q}{1 - \bar{P}Q}\right|, then render the Euclidean circle with center CEC_E and radius RER_E.

  • Center + Radius / Transfer Distance (Non-collapsible Compass): Given hyperbolic center PP and either a numeric radius ρ\rho or two reference points A,BA, B defining radius ρ=d(A,B)\rho = d(A, B), construct the hyperbolic circle centered at PP with that exact hyperbolic radius.

Hyperbolic Line (Straightedge)

Through any two distinct points P,Q∈𝔻P, Q \in \mathbb{D}, there is a unique hyperbolic geodesic:

  • If P,QP, Q, and the origin are collinear (PxQy−PyQx=0P_x Q_y - P_y Q_x = 0), the line is the Euclidean diameter passing through PP and QQ.

  • Otherwise, reflect PP across the unit circle via circle inversion to obtain P′=P/|P|2P' = P / |P|^2. The unique Euclidean circle through P,QP, Q, and P′P' intersects the unit circle orthogonally (|CL|2=RL2+1|C_L|^2 = R_L^2 + 1). The arc inside 𝔻\mathbb{D} forms the hyperbolic line, and the arc between PP and QQ forms the hyperbolic segment.

5. Isometries & Möbius Transformations

Hyperbolic space is homogeneous: every point has identical local geometry. Translations and rotations are represented by disk automorphisms (Möbius transformations):

z↦eiθz−a1−a‾z,|a|<1,θ∈ℝz \mapsto e^{i\theta} \frac{z - a}{1 - \bar{a} z}, \quad |a| < 1, \; \theta \in \mathbb{R}

These transformations preserve hyperbolic distances, circles, and conformal angles, allowing any point in the disk to be smoothly translated to the center for inspection without metric distortion.
