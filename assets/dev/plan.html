Constructors: Design & Implementation Plan

1. Architectural Model & Taxonomy

A Constructor is an interactive, parameterized command that creates one or more geometric objects (SceneObject) and their generating Constraint node in the bipartite constraint graph.

Constructors are classified into two categories:

  • Primitive Constructors (Single-Constraint Generators): Directly construct an object driven by a dedicated Constraint node in the bipartite DAG (e.g., PointObject via FreePointConstraint, LineObject via TwoPointLineConstraint, CircleObject via CircleRadiusConstraint).

  • Compound / Macro Constructors (Multi-Constraint Generators): Synthesize high-level geometric constructions (e.g., perpendicular bisector, circumcircle) by executing a sequence of primitive constructions. Compound constructors wire intermediate objects and return primary output objects to the scene. They can either remain as dynamic subgraphs (where moving original points moves all intermediate and derived objects) or be collapsed into a composite constraint.

Constructors are grouped by their primary output type:

  • Points: Free points, points on curves, intersections, midpoints.

  • Lines, Rays & Segments: Full geodesics, segments, rays, angle lines, perpendiculars.

  • Circles: Center and passing point, center and radius, compass transfer.

  • Numbers & Measurements: Hyperbolic distances, angles, coordinates, free sliders.

  • Constructions (High School & Neutral Geometry): Perpendicular bisectors, angle bisectors, dropping perpendiculars, copying angles/segments, polygons.

2. User Interaction & Workflow Design

Multi-Step Selection State Machine

Constructors that require multiple inputs (e.g., two points for a line, or a line and an external point for a perpendicular) follow a standardized state machine:

  1. Activation: Clicking a constructor tool in the toolbar (or selecting it from a group dropdown) activates the tool and enters step 1 of its selection sequence.

  2. Contextual Status & Prompt: A banner or status line above the canvas displays prompt instructions (e.g., "Select first point", "Select line", "Select distance or enter radius").

  3. Visual Affordances & Snapping: Hovering over candidate objects highlights them with an outline or glow. If a constructor expects a point, clicking in empty canvas space can either instantly create a new FreePoint or snap to an existing point or intersection.

  4. Chaining & Nesting (Stacked Input Creation): If a constructor requires an input (e.g., a point) that does not yet exist, the user can seamlessly invoke a point constructor or click on an intersection to create that input inline without losing their place in the parent constructor sequence.

  5. Completion & Selection: Once all inputs are satisfied, the output objects and constraint are committed to SceneData, added to MyUndoManager as a single undoable action, and automatically selected.

  6. Cancellation: Pressing Escape, clicking the Select tool, or clicking a dedicated Cancel action cancels the active constructor and resets selection state.

Toolbar Organization & Progressive Disclosure

  • Grouped Dropdown / Flyout Buttons: To prevent toolbar clutter, the toolbar organizes constructors into five dropdown clusters: Points, Lines, Circles, Measurements, and Constructions. Each button displays the active constructor icon, with a dropdown arrow revealing alternative constructors in that family.

  • Progressive Disclosure via Tutorials: New users start with a clean, focused default toolset (Free Point, Geodesic Line, Segment, Circle by Center+Point, Distance, Move Disk, Select). Advanced constructors (Perpendicular Bisector, Angle Bisector, Limiting Parallels, Inscribed Circle) are progressively unlocked as the user completes relevant tutorials, or can be toggled on all at once via a “Show All Tools” setting.

Object Naming, Styling & Number Ranges

  • Name Suggestion: Default names follow mathematical conventions via NameGuesser (points: A,B,C…A, B, C \dots; lines: L1,L2…L_1, L_2 \dots; circles: C1,C2…C_1, C_2 \dots; angles: α,β,θ\alpha, \beta, \theta; distances: d1,d(A,B)d_1, d(A, B)).

  • Color Inheritance: New objects automatically adopt the currently selected palette color in the toolbar.

  • Number Range Configuration: Every created NumberObject defaults to reasonable bounds based on its role:

    • Hyperbolic distance: 0≤d<∞0 \le d < \infty (default range [0,5][0, 5], editable).

    • Angle: [0,π][0, \pi] or [0,2π][0, 2\pi] with radians/degrees toggle.

    • Free parameters: customizable minimum, maximum, and step size.

3. Detailed Constructor Catalog

A. Points

  • Free Point:

    • Inputs: Screen click converted to Poincarédisk coordinates (ux,uy)(u_x, u_y).

    • Outputs: PointObject governed by FreePointConstraint.

  • Point on Line / Segment / Ray:

    • Inputs: Target line LL, initial click position near LL.

    • Outputs: PointObject constrained by parameter t∈ℝt \in \mathbb{R} on LL.

  • Point on Circle:

    • Inputs: Target circle CC, initial click position near CC.

    • Outputs: PointObject constrained by angle θ∈[0,2π)\theta \in [0, 2\pi) on CC.

  • Intersection Point(s):

    • Inputs: Two curves (Line-Line, Line-Circle, Circle-Circle).

    • Outputs: 1 or 2 PointObjects governed by IntersectionConstraint.

  • Midpoint:

    • Inputs: Two points A,BA, B or segment ABAB.

    • Outputs: PointObject at hyperbolic midpoint MM such that d(A,M)=d(M,B)d(A, M) = d(M, B).

B. Lines, Rays & Segments

  • Line through Two Points:

    • Inputs: Two points A,BA, B.

    • Outputs: Complete geodesic LineObject extending to boundary circle.

  • Segment between Two Points:

    • Inputs: Two points A,BA, B.

    • Outputs: Bounded geodesic LineObject (segment) between AA and BB.

  • Ray through Two Points:

    • Inputs: Endpoint point AA, direction point BB.

    • Outputs: Semi-infinite geodesic LineObject (ray) starting at AA through BB to boundary.

  • Line by Point and Angle:

    • Inputs: Base line LL, point P∈LP \in L, angle NumberObject θ\theta.

    • Outputs: Geodesic line through PP making angle θ\theta with LL.

  • Extend Segment to Line:

    • Inputs: Segment or Ray.

    • Outputs: Full geodesic line through the segment’s endpoints.

C. Circles

  • Circle by Center and Passing Point:

    • Inputs: Center point PP, boundary point QQ.

    • Outputs: CircleObject with radius d(P,Q)d(P, Q).

  • Circle by Center and Radius Number:

    • Inputs: Center point PP, NumberObject ρ\rho.

    • Outputs: CircleObject with hyperbolic radius ρ\rho.

  • Compass Circle (Transfer Distance):

    • Inputs: Segment ABAB (or pair of points A,BA, B), center point PP.

    • Outputs: CircleObject centered at PP with hyperbolic radius equal to d(A,B)d(A, B).

D. Numbers & Measurements

  • Free Slider Number:

    • Inputs: Value, min, max (via dialog or default slider).

    • Outputs: Free NumberObject displayed in side panel.

  • Distance Measurement:

    • Inputs: Two points A,BA, B.

    • Outputs: NumberObject constrained to d(A,B)=2artanh⁡|A−B1−A‾B|d(A, B) = 2\operatorname{artanh}\left|\frac{A - B}{1 - \bar{A}B}\right|.

  • Angle Measurement:

    • Inputs: Three points A,B,CA, B, C (vertex at BB) or two intersecting lines.

    • Outputs: NumberObject constrained to conformal hyperbolic angle ∠ABC\angle ABC.

  • Point Coordinates:

    • Inputs: Point PP.

    • Outputs: Linked NumberObjects for (x,y)(x, y) or polar (r,θ)(r, \theta).

E. High School Geometry Constructions (Neutral & Classical)

In hyperbolic geometry, all neutral geometry constructions (Euclid’s Book I, propositions 1–28) hold identically using straightedge and compass:

  • Perpendicular Bisector of Segment:

    • Inputs: Segment ABAB (or two points A,BA, B).

    • Construction: Intersect two equal-radius circles centered at AA and BB; draw geodesic line through intersection points.

    • Outputs: Midpoint MM, perpendicular bisector line L⟂L_\perp.

  • Angle Bisector:

    • Inputs: Angle ∠ABC\angle ABC (three points or two intersecting lines).

    • Construction: Circle centered at BB finds equidistant points P1∈BA,P2∈BCP_1 \in BA, P_2 \in BC; intersecting circles at P1,P2P_1, P_2 yield point QQ; line BQBQ bisects ∠ABC\angle ABC.

    • Outputs: Bisector ray/line.

  • Perpendicular through Point on Line:

    • Inputs: Line LL, point P∈LP \in L.

    • Outputs: Geodesic line orthogonal to LL at PP.

  • Perpendicular from External Point to Line (Drop Perpendicular):

    • Inputs: Line LL, point P∉LP \notin L.

    • Construction: Circle centered at PP meets LL at two points; perpendicular bisector of those points passes through PP and is orthogonal to LL.

    • Outputs: Orthogonal foot point H∈LH \in L, perpendicular line PHPH.

  • Copy Segment (Compass Transfer):

    • Inputs: Segment ABAB, target ray or line with origin OO.

    • Outputs: Point PP on target ray such that d(O,P)=d(A,B)d(O, P) = d(A, B).

  • Copy Angle:

    • Inputs: Angle ∠ABC\angle ABC, target ray with vertex VV.

    • Outputs: Ray from VV forming an identical angle.

  • Equilateral Triangle:

    • Inputs: Segment ABAB.

    • Outputs: Point CC (intersection of circles of radius ABAB at AA and BB) and triangle segments AC,BCAC, BC.

  • Circumscribed Circle (Circumcenter):

    • Inputs: Three non-collinear points A,B,CA, B, C.

    • Outputs: Circumcenter OO (intersection of perpendicular bisectors) and circumcircle passing through A,B,CA, B, C (when circumcenter exists in 𝔻\mathbb{D}).

  • Inscribed Circle (Incenter):

    • Inputs: Triangle A,B,CA, B, C.

    • Outputs: Incenter II (intersection of angle bisectors) and incircle tangent to all three edges.

F. Hyperbolic-Specific Constructions

  • Limiting Parallel Line (Asymptotic Geodesic):

    • Inputs: Line LL, external point P∉LP \notin L, direction (left/right).

    • Construction: Find ideal endpoint Ω∈∂𝔻\Omega \in \partial \mathbb{D} of LL; draw geodesic line through PP and Ω\Omega.

    • Outputs: Limiting parallel line meeting LL at infinity.

  • Common Perpendicular of Ultraparallel Lines:

    • Inputs: Two ultraparallel (non-intersecting, non-asymptotic) lines L1,L2L_1, L_2.

    • Construction: Determine the unique geodesic orthogonal to both lines.

    • Outputs: Orthogonal geodesic line and the two foot points of shortest distance.

  • Ideal Triangle:

    • Inputs: Three ideal boundary points Ω1,Ω2,Ω3∈∂𝔻\Omega_1, \Omega_2, \Omega_3 \in \partial \mathbb{D}.

    • Outputs: Three bounding geodesic lines, forming a triangle with angle sum 00.

  • Lambert & Saccheri Quadrilaterals:

    • Inputs: Base segment ABAB, height hh.

    • Outputs: Quadrilateral with right angles at AA and BB, equal legs AD=BC=hAD = BC = h, and acute summit angles at CC and DD.

UI & Viewport Navigation

The main drawing canvas will render the Poincaréunit disk with points, lines, line segments/rays, and circles drawn on it.

  • Viewport Navigation: The canvas will support both:

    • Euclidean Zoom & Pan: Allows inspecting fine details near the unit disk boundary where hyperbolic objects crowd together in Euclidean space.

    • Hyperbolic Translation (Möbius Isometry Mode): Applies disk automorphisms z↦eiθz−a1−a‾zz \mapsto e^{i\theta}\frac{z - a}{1 - \bar{a}z} to smoothly shift which point of the hyperbolic plane is at the center of the unit disk while preserving all hyperbolic distances and angles.

  • Number Objects Panel: There will be a scrollable list of Number objects. Depending on the aspect ratio of the window (using SplitterWidget), the list will be positioned below the disk (portrait/narrow) or to the right of the disk (landscape/wide).

Objects

As in spacetime, most objects can have names, colors, visibility, and locked states.

  • Geometric Objects (drawn on the main canvas):

    • Point: A point z∈𝔻z \in \mathbb{D} inside the open unit disk.

    • Line / Segment / Ray: A hyperbolic geodesic (orthogonal circular arc or diameter), rendered as a full line across the disk, a segment between two points, or a ray.

    • Circle: A hyperbolic circle with hyperbolic center PP and hyperbolic radius ρ\rho.

  • Number Objects (displayed in the side/bottom list):

    • Represent real numbers (coordinates, hyperbolic distances/radii, angles, or free parameters).

    • When editing a Number object, an interactive slider (MySlider) lets the user smoothly adjust its value and animate it over a range.

  • Locking: Objects can be locked so their values/positions cannot be adjusted by direct manipulation or by the constraint solver.

  • Constraints (Bipartite Object/Constraint Graph):

    • Every object is generated or governed by a Constraint, forming an alternating (red/black) bipartite graph of Drawable/Number objects and Constraint nodes:

      • Free / Parameter Constraints: Free point (x,y)(x, y) or polar (r,θ)(r, \theta) driven by two Number objects; point constrained to lie on a line or circle at parameter tt.

      • Line Constraints: Line/segment/ray through two points P1,P2P_1, P_2.

      • Angle Constraint: A line through a point PP on another line LL that forms an angle with LL whose measure is specified by a Number object (note: because the Poincarédisk model is conformal, hyperbolic angles equal the Euclidean angles between tangent vectors at PP).

      • Circle Constraints: Circle from center PP and point QQ on circle; circle from center PP and Number radius ρ\rho (or distance d(A,B)d(A, B) between two points).

      • Intersection Constraints: Intersection point(s) of two lines, a line and a circle, or two circles.

      • Measurement / Coordinate Constraints: Number constrained to be the hyperbolic distance d(P,Q)d(P, Q), hyperbolic angle ∠ABC\angle ABC, or disk coordinates (x,y,r,θ)(x, y, r, \theta) of a point.

Constraint Solver

When the user selects and drags an object (or adjusts a Number slider):

  1. Downward Propagation (Forward Evaluation): Changes to ancestor inputs automatically recompute all descendant constraints and objects in topological order.

  2. Upward Propagation (Numerical Inverse Solver): If the user drags a derived object (such as the intersection of two lines or a point on a circle) whose inputs are not locked, the solver traverses upward to identify unlocked ancestor parameters and takes a damped least-squares / gradient descent step (using the Jacobian of the dragged object’s position with respect to unlocked ancestor parameters) so the dragged object follows the user’s cursor while keeping the entire constraint graph satisfied.

Macro / Tutorials

There will be a macro system unified with the tutorial system (lib/script.dart and lib/tutorial_widget.dart from spacetime). When a user runs a macro, it executes a sequence of steps, pausing to prompt the user to select or place required input objects.

For example, to construct a line perpendicular to a given line LL through a point PP:

  1. Find/place two points A,BA, B on the line LL.

  2. Construct two circles centered at AA and BB passing through PP (or two equal-radius circles from PP intersecting LL at A,BA, B and then equal-radius circles around AA and BB).

  3. Find the intersection points of those two circles.

  4. Construct the hyperbolic line through those intersection points.

Proposed Standard Tutorials (For User Review)

  1. 01_welcome_to_poincare.md - Welcome to the PoincaréDisk

    • Concepts: The unit disk 𝔻={z∈ℂ:|z|<1}\mathbb{D} = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : |z| < 1\}, boundary circle at infinity ∂𝔻\partial \mathbb{D}, and geodesic lines (circular arcs orthogonal to the boundary circle and Euclidean diameters).

    • Activities: Place points, draw geodesic lines and segments, and inspect orthogonal intersections with the boundary circle.

    • Takeaway: Hyperbolic lines appear curved to Euclidean eyes, but inside hyperbolic space they represent straight, shortest-path geodesics.

  2. 02_exponential_distance.md - Distance & The Edge of the Universe

    • Concepts: The hyperbolic distance metric d(u,v)=2artanh⁡|u−v1−u‾v|d(u, v) = 2 \operatorname{artanh}\left|\frac{u - v}{1 - \bar{u}v}\right| and exponential metric expansion near the boundary circle.

    • Activities: Measure distances with the Distance measurement tool, drag points closer to the boundary, and adjust numeric sliders.

    • Takeaway: The boundary circle is infinitely far away; an infinite universe is represented faithfully inside a finite drawing.

  3. 03_mobius_translations.md - Hyperbolic Translations & Homogeneity

    • Concepts: Möbius disk automorphisms z↦z−a1−a‾zz \mapsto \frac{z - a}{1 - \bar{a}z}. Hyperbolic space is homogeneous: every point has identical geometry.

    • Activities: Use the Move Disk tool to translate an off-center cluster to the origin; observe the preservation of hyperbolic lengths and conformal angles.

    • Takeaway: Apparent boundary distortion is an artifact of Euclidean projection; any point can be centered and viewed without distortion.

  4. 04_hyperbolic_circles.md - Circles with Shifted Centers

    • Concepts: Hyperbolic circles are Euclidean circles, but the Euclidean center CEC_E is shifted closer to the origin than the true hyperbolic center PP: CE=1−t21−p2t2PC_E = \frac{1 - t^2}{1 - p^2 t^2} P.

    • Activities: Construct hyperbolic circles by center and radius, compare hyperbolic vs Euclidean radius, and translate the center to the origin to observe CEC_E and PP align.

    • Takeaway: The metric gradient shifts the Euclidean center while the hyperbolic radius remains constant in all directions.

  5. 05_parallel_postulate.md - The Failure of Euclid’s Fifth Postulate

    • Concepts: The core difference between Euclidean and hyperbolic geometry. Through a point P∉LP \notin L, there are infinitely many non-intersecting lines.

    • Activities: Given line LL and point PP, construct the two limiting parallels (asymptotic rays meeting LL at the boundary circle) and the fan of divergent ultraparallel lines between them.

    • Takeaway: Directly experiencing the non-Euclidean property that launched modern geometry.

  6. 06_triangles_and_defect.md - Triangles & The Angle Defect

    • Concepts: The angle sum in hyperbolic triangles is strictly less than 180∘180^\circ (∑α<π\sum \alpha < \pi). Area is directly proportional to the angle defect: Area=π−(α+β+γ)\text{Area} = \pi - (\alpha + \beta + \gamma).

    • Activities: Construct a triangle ABCABC, measure the three angles with the Angle tool, drag vertices to vary the triangle’s size, and construct an ideal triangle with all vertices on the boundary circle (∑α=0\sum \alpha = 0).

    • Takeaway: There are no similar triangles of different sizes in hyperbolic geometry; scale determines shape and area.

  7. 07_perpendicular_bisector.md - Classical Compass Constructions

    • Concepts: Classical straightedge-and-compass constructions in hyperbolic geometry using intersecting circles.

    • Activities: Construct the perpendicular bisector and midpoint of a segment ABAB by intersecting equal-radius hyperbolic circles centered at AA and BB.

    • Takeaway: Neutral geometry constructions from Euclid’s Book I hold without modification in hyperbolic space.

  8. 08_saccheri_quadrilaterals.md - Saccheri & Lambert Quadrilaterals

    • Concepts: Quadrilaterals with right angles and equal legs whose summit angles are strictly acute (<90∘< 90^\circ).

    • Activities: Construct a Saccheri quadrilateral, measure summit angles, and observe how the summit curve diverges from a geodesic.

    • Takeaway: Retracing the historical investigations of Omar Khayyam, Saccheri, and Lambert.
